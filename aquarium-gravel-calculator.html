How much gravel for an aquarium?

Aquarium gravel is estimated from the footprint of the tank (length × width) and the substrate depth, not the water volume. Common depths:

  • 1–2 inches for a basic gravel bed or non-planted tank.
  • 2–3 inches for rooted live plants so roots can anchor.

Volume = Length × Width × Substrate depth

Weight = Volume × substrate density

Quick rule of thumb

A widely used estimate is about 1 to 1.5 lb of gravel per US gallon for a roughly 2 inch bed. For a deeper planted substrate, use closer to 2 lb per gallon. The calculator above uses your actual tank dimensions for a more accurate figure.

Frequently asked questions

How much gravel do I need for a fish tank?

Estimate from the tank's length × width × substrate depth. A common rule is about 1–1.5 lb of gravel per US gallon for a 2 inch bed. Enter your tank size above for an exact figure.

How deep should aquarium gravel be?

1–2 inches for a basic substrate, or 2–3 inches for live rooted plants so the roots can anchor and access nutrients.

How much gravel for a 20 gallon tank?

Roughly 15–20 lb for a standard 20 gallon (24 × 12 in) tank at a 2 inch depth. Use more for a planted tank.

Is aquarium gravel measured by water volume?

No — it is based on the tank's footprint (length × width) and the substrate depth, not the gallons of water. The per-gallon rule is just a quick approximation.
