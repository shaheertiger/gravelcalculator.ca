How do you figure how much asphalt you need?

Paving plants sell hot-mix asphalt by the tonne, so the job is to turn your area and thickness into weight:

  1. Measure length × width to get the area (ft² or m²).
  2. Pick the compacted thickness — not the loose depth before rolling.
  3. Volume = area × thickness (convert inches to feet, or cm to metres).
  4. Tonnes = volume (m³) × 2.32. One cubic yard of compacted asphalt is about 1.77 tonnes.

Tonnes = Length (m) × Width (m) × Thickness (m) × 2.32

Imperial shortcut: ft² × inches thick × 12 lb ≈ pounds of asphalt (÷ 2,205 for tonnes)

Example

A 12 × 50 ft driveway is 600 ft². At 2" compacted: 600 × 2 ÷ 12 = 100 ft³ = 3.7 yd³ = 2.83 m³. Then 2.83 × 2.32 ≈ 6.6 tonnes. Add 5% for edges and waste and order about 7 tonnes.

Is asphalt the same as bitumen?

Not quite. Bitumen is the black binder; asphalt (hot mix, blacktop) is about 5% bitumen and 95% stone and sand. Driveway quotes are for asphalt by the tonne, so calculate asphalt, not bitumen. Cold-patch bags are sold by weight, often 25–30 kg each: one tonne is roughly 35–40 bags.

Frequently asked questions

How many tonnes of asphalt do I need for a driveway?

Multiply area by compacted thickness and by 2.32 t/m³. A 12 × 50 ft driveway at 2 inches needs about 6.5 tonnes; at 3 inches, about 10 tonnes.

How much does a cubic yard of asphalt weigh?

About 1.77 tonnes (roughly 3,900 lb) once compacted. Loose hot mix in the truck is lighter per yard because it hasn't been rolled.

How many square feet does a tonne of asphalt cover?

About 90 ft² at 2 inches thick, or about 60 ft² at 3 inches.

What is the formula for figuring asphalt?

Tonnes = length × width × thickness (all in metres) × 2.32. In imperial units, ft² × inches ÷ 12 ÷ 27 gives cubic yards; multiply by 1.77 for tonnes.

Is bitumen the same as asphalt?

No. Bitumen is the binder; asphalt is stone and sand bound with about 5% bitumen. Paving is priced and ordered as asphalt by the tonne.
