Bulk Gravel vs Bagged Gravel: Which Is Cheaper?

Published June 10, 2026 · Updated June 19, 2026 · GravelCalculator.ca

For anything bigger than a small garden bed, bulk gravel is almost always cheaper than bagged — often by 2–4× per unit. Here's the cost comparison, the break-even point, and when bags still make sense.

The cost difference

Bagged gravel is convenient but you pay for the packaging, handling and store markup. Bulk gravel is sold loose by the tonne or cubic yard at a landscape-supply yard or quarry.

FormatTypical price (CAD)Effective cost per yd³
Bagged, 0.5 ft³ bag$5 – $9 per bag≈ $270 – $490 / yd³
Bagged, 30 kg bag$6 – $10 per bag≈ $200 – $350 / yd³
Bulk, picked up$20 – $70 per yd³$20 – $70 / yd³
Bulk, delivered$40 – $100 per yd³$40 – $100 / yd³

It takes about 40 bags of 0.5 ft³ to make a single cubic yard (27 ft³). That's why bags add up fast.

The break-even point

Bulk usually wins once your project reaches about 1 cubic yard (roughly 1.2 tonnes). Below that, the delivery fee or minimum order on bulk can make bags competitive — especially if you only need a bag or two and can carry them home.

As a rule of thumb:

  • Under ~½ yard: bags are convenient and often fine.
  • ½ to 1 yard: compare the bulk delivery minimum against the bag count.
  • Over 1 yard: buy bulk — the savings are large.

When bagged gravel still makes sense

  • Small or one-off jobs (a planter, a drainage patch, a few pavers).
  • No way to receive a bulk delivery or store a loose pile.
  • You need a clean, specific decorative stone in a small amount.
  • Access is tight and you'd be barrowing anyway.

Frequently asked questions

Is bulk gravel cheaper than bagged?

Yes, usually much cheaper. Bagged gravel can work out to $200–$490 per cubic yard, while bulk gravel is often $20–$100 per cubic yard. Bulk wins for any project over about one cubic yard.

How many bags of gravel make a cubic yard?

About 40 bags of 0.5 ft³, or 20 bags of 1 ft³, since a cubic yard is 27 cubic feet. By weight, roughly 40–45 bags of 30 kg gravel equal one cubic yard.

When should I buy bagged gravel instead of bulk?

For small jobs under about half a cubic yard, when you can't take a bulk delivery, or when you need a small amount of a specific decorative stone. Otherwise bulk is cheaper.

Is bulk gravel worth the delivery fee?

For projects over roughly one cubic yard, yes — the per-unit savings on bulk usually more than cover delivery. For very small jobs, the delivery minimum can make bags competitive.
