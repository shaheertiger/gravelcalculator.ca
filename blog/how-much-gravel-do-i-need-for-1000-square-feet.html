How Much Gravel Do I Need for 1,000 Square Feet?

Published June 22, 2026 · GravelCalculator.ca

Covering 1,000 ft² takes about 6.2 cubic yards (9 tonnes) at 2 inches deep. Here is the full breakdown by depth, in cubic yards and tonnes, plus the cost to budget for.

Gravel for 1,000 ft² by depth

A cubic yard covers 27 ft² at 12 inches, so 1,000 ft² takes the following at common depths:

DepthCubic yardsCubic metresTonnes
2 in (5 cm)≈ 6.2 yd³≈ 4.7 m³≈ 9.0 t
3 in (7.5 cm)≈ 9.3 yd³≈ 7.1 m³≈ 13.5 t
4 in (10 cm)≈ 12.3 yd³≈ 9.4 m³≈ 18.0 t
6 in (15 cm)≈ 18.5 yd³≈ 14.2 m³≈ 27.0 t

Tonnage assumes a typical density of about 1.5 t/m³. Add 5–10% extra for compaction and uneven ground.

What will it cost?

At roughly CAD $20–$70 per cubic yard for bulk gravel, 1,000 ft² at 2 inches (about 6.2 yd³) runs around $125–$435 in material, before delivery. A 4 inch layer roughly doubles that. Decorative stone such as river rock or coloured gravel costs more.

How the math works

Multiply area by depth (in the same units), then convert: 1,000 ft² × 2 in ÷ 12 = 166.7 ft³, and 166.7 ÷ 27 ≈ 6.2 yd³. To skip the arithmetic, enter your numbers in the Cubic Yards of Gravel Calculator or the Aggregate Calculator. See also how much a tonne of gravel covers.

Frequently asked questions

How many yards of gravel do I need for 1,000 square feet?

About 6.2 cubic yards at 2 inches deep, 9.3 yards at 3 inches, and 12.3 yards at 4 inches. Multiply area by depth in feet, then divide by 27 to get cubic yards.

How many tonnes of gravel for 1,000 square feet?

Roughly 9 tonnes at 2 inches, 13.5 tonnes at 3 inches, and 18 tonnes at 4 inches, using a typical gravel density of about 1.5 t/m³. Add 5–10% for compaction.

How much does it cost to gravel 1,000 square feet?

At about $20–$70 per cubic yard for bulk gravel, a 2 inch layer over 1,000 ft² costs roughly $125–$435 in material before delivery. A 4 inch layer is about double, and decorative stone costs more.

How deep should gravel be for a 1,000 sq ft area?

It depends on use: 2 inches for decorative ground cover, 3–4 inches for paths, and 4–6 inches of compacted base for driveways or parking. Deeper layers need proportionally more gravel.
