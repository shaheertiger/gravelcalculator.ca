How Many Tonnes of Gravel Are in a Cubic Yard?

Published June 6, 2026 · Updated June 19, 2026 · GravelCalculator.ca

A cubic yard of gravel weighs roughly 1.1 to 1.5 tonnes (about 1.2 to 1.7 US tons) — the exact figure depends on the gravel's density. Here's the conversion, a quick-reference table, and how to do it for any material.

The short answer

1 cubic yard of typical gravel ≈ 1.2–1.4 tonnes. Loose dry gravel is on the lighter side (≈1.16 t/yd³) and pea gravel or wet gravel on the heavier side (≈1.4–1.5 t/yd³).

The conversion formula

Weight comes from volume × density. One cubic yard equals 0.7646 cubic metres, so:

Tonnes per yd³ = 0.7646 × Density (t/m³)

For crusher run at 1.60 t/m³: 0.7646 × 1.60 ≈ 1.22 tonnes per cubic yard.

Going the other way: tonnes to cubic yards

To turn a tonnage back into yards, divide by the same factor. For crusher run, 1 tonne ÷ 1.22 ≈ 0.82 cubic yards. As a rough rule, 1 tonne of gravel ≈ 0.7–0.9 cubic yards.

Frequently asked questions

How many tonnes are in a cubic yard of gravel?

About 1.1–1.5 tonnes, depending on the gravel. Typical crushed stone is roughly 1.22 tonnes per cubic yard; pea gravel and wet gravel are heavier at about 1.4–1.5 tonnes per yard.

How do I convert cubic yards of gravel to tonnes?

Multiply cubic yards by 0.7646 to get cubic metres, then multiply by the gravel's density in t/m³. Or just multiply cubic yards by about 1.2–1.4 for typical gravel.

How many cubic yards are in a tonne of gravel?

Roughly 0.7–0.9 cubic yards per tonne for typical gravel. Denser or wet gravel gives fewer yards per tonne; loose dry gravel gives slightly more.

Why does the weight of a cubic yard of gravel vary?

Because density changes with the stone's mineral type, particle size, how tightly it packs and how wet it is. Wet gravel can weigh 20–30% more than the same volume when dry.
