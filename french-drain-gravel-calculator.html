How much gravel for a French drain?

A French drain is a trench filled with washed clear stone around a perforated pipe. To estimate the gravel, treat the trench as a long rectangle:

  1. Measure the trench length and width (often 12 inches / 30 cm wide).
  2. Set the depth of gravel around the pipe (commonly 8–12 inches once the pipe is bedded).
  3. The calculator returns the volume in cubic yards and tonnes.

Gravel volume = Length × Width × Depth

Tips

  • Use washed clear stone (0–3/4" clear / 20 mm clear) with no fines so water drains freely.
  • Wrap the trench and stone in landscape/filter fabric to keep soil from clogging the drain.
  • Subtract the pipe volume if you want a tighter estimate, or just round up — extra stone is useful.

Frequently asked questions

How much gravel do I need for a French drain?

Multiply the trench length × width × gravel depth. A 30 ft trench, 1 ft wide with 8 inches of stone needs about 20 ft³ ≈ 0.75 cubic yards (about 1 tonne). Use the calculator for your dimensions.

What gravel is best for a French drain?

Washed clear stone (0–3/4 inch / 20 mm clear) with no fines, so water passes through freely. Avoid crusher run, which has fines that compact and reduce drainage.

How deep should a French drain be?

Typically 18–24 inches deep overall, with 8–12 inches of clear stone around a perforated pipe, sloped about 1% to carry water away.

Should I wrap the gravel in fabric?

Yes — line the trench with filter fabric so surrounding soil does not migrate into and clog the stone over time.
